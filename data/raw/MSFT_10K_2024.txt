ITEM 1. BUSINESS

General
Microsoft is a technology company committed to making digital technology and artificial intelligence available broadly. Founded in 1975, the company develops and supports software, services, devices, and solutions.

Operating Segments
Microsoft operates and reports financial performance using three segments: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing.

Productivity and Business Processes primarily comprises Office Commercial (Office 365, Microsoft Teams, Copilot for Microsoft 365), Office Consumer, LinkedIn, and Dynamics business solutions.

Intelligent Cloud primarily comprises server products and cloud services, including Azure, SQL Server, Windows Server, and GitHub, plus Enterprise and Partner Services.

More Personal Computing primarily comprises Windows (OEM and Commercial licensing), Devices (Surface, HoloLens), Gaming (Xbox hardware, content, and services including Activision Blizzard, which joined Microsoft in October 2023), and Search and news advertising (Bing, Copilot, Microsoft Edge).

Competition
Azure faces competition from Amazon, Google, IBM, Oracle, and open source offerings. AI offerings compete with products from Amazon and Google, as well as Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and other open source offerings, many of which are also current or potential partners.
Windows faces competition mainly from Apple and Google. Xbox and cloud gaming face competition from Amazon, Apple, Meta, and Tencent, and console competition from Nintendo and Sony.

Human Capital
As of June 30, 2024, Microsoft employed approximately 228,000 people on a full-time basis: 126,000 in the U.S. and 102,000 internationally. Of these, 86,000 were in operations, 81,000 in product research and development, 45,000 in sales and marketing, and 16,000 in general and administration.

Research and Development
Microsoft currently has a portfolio of over 63,000 U.S. and international patents issued and over 23,000 pending worldwide. Engineering groups include Cloud and AI, Strategic Missions and Technologies, Experiences and Devices, Microsoft AI, Microsoft Security, Technology and Research (including Microsoft Research), LinkedIn, and Gaming.

ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS

Strategic and Competitive Risks
Microsoft faces intense competition across all markets. Barriers to entry in many of Microsoft's businesses are low, and the company competes against platform-based ecosystems, vertically-integrated competitors, and open-source alternatives.
Microsoft is investing in AI across the entire company and infusing generative AI capabilities into consumer and commercial offerings. Microsoft expects AI technology and services to be a highly competitive and rapidly evolving market, and will bear significant development and operational costs to build AI models, services, platforms, and infrastructure.

Cybersecurity, Data Privacy, and Platform Abuse Risks
Microsoft has experienced cybersecurity incidents in which threat actors gained unauthorized access to IT systems and data. As disclosed in a Form 8-K filed January 19, 2024 (amended March 8, 2024), beginning in late November 2023, a nation-state associated threat actor used a password spray attack to compromise a legacy test account and gain access to Microsoft email accounts, and used this to attempt unauthorized access to some source code repositories and internal systems.

Issues in the development and use of AI may result in reputational or competitive harm or liability. AI algorithms or training methodologies may be flawed, datasets may be biased, and content generated by AI systems may be offensive, illegal, inaccurate, or otherwise harmful. Risks may arise from copyright infringement claims related to AI training and output, and new regulations such as the EU AI Act and the U.S. AI Executive Order.

Legal, Regulatory, and Litigation Risks
The European Commission has designated Windows and LinkedIn as core platform services subject to obligations under the EU Digital Markets Act.
Acquisitions: Microsoft completed its acquisition of Nuance Communications in March 2022 and Activision Blizzard in October 2023. The Federal Trade Commission continues to challenge the Activision Blizzard acquisition and could, if successful, alter or unwind the transaction.
